about

Music has always felt like home. I grew up in Alaska, and started playing guitar in 5th grade in rock and jazz bands. That was back when you listened to the radio in the car and full-length albums at home. My earliest influences came from the classic rock radio station in Anchorage and from my dad blasting his favorite albums on tape and cds. The radio station had a Three-Fer Thursday segment that featured three continuous tracks from a single record, radio host's choice. My dad introduced me to the beauty and the chaos of the album as a music format, and I still know albums like Led Zeppelin I, Dark Side of the Moon, Quadrophrenia, American Beauty, Zappa's Over-Nite/Sensation, the White Album, and 2112 inside out because of it.

But I've since come to realize that I've always been on a search for sound and sonic experiences. With the rise of the Strokes and the 3rd British Invasion during my formative early-teen years, albums like Back to Black, Is This It?, and Whatever People Say I Am That's What I'm Not captured my imagination. So did the big-band chaos of Charles Mingus, Dizzie Gillespie, and Miles Davis. The beauty, wit, radicalism, and raw emotion of singers like Nina Simone and Roberta Flack. I loved music with a polished edge. I still do. 

I floated around in the late aughts to the mid 2010s during college. I was worried that I wasn't as talented a musician as a lot of other people. I wrote music. I played a couple of gigs at small house parties, and I went to a lot of shows in the Twin Cities. I was just totally caught up in my own head about my voice and talent-level that I didn't want to give it a go. My "ol' college try" was to keep my music confined in my bedroom and in my head. A lot of these experiences, whether the few-and-far-between gigs I played or the shows I went out to, left me wanting more. But I didn't understand what I wanted until after college.

It turned out what I wanted was an incredibly dark room with a perfect four-point sound system that literally rattles your insides. The IYKYK techno party at a warehouse in North Minneapolis changed everything for me. The music was heady, at times abrasive, yet I couldn't stop myself from dancing. Later, I'd come to find out that the music was house and techno--a lot of techno. But at that time, without the language to describe what I listened to, when the environment was purely experiential, I heard jazz and soul and rock and pop. I heard groove. I heard that polished edge I can't get enough of. And I locked in. I've never been a dancer, but holy shit, I left with buckets of sweat pouring off me. 

The night before I moved to Denver, I was at that techno party at that warehouse in North Minneapolis. It turned me upside down. In Denver, I went out to a lot of dance music events. I bought a drum machine. I was afraid to give up the guitar so I bought a looping pedal and a bass synth pedal to try and make my guitar sound like what I was hearing at the party. Musically, I felt adrift.

When I moved back to Minneapolis just before the COVID pandemic shut down the world, I was almost ready to give up making music entirely. Shows and dancing at parties would be enough as I tried to fit into the fictitous world of professional adulthood.

Amid the sludge of the pandemic and the chaos of the uprising in Minneapolis, music was able to deliver me a lot of peace. I started making music again and I started collecting records for the first time in my life. I learned how to use Ableton, and I struggled through mixing and mastering my first EP. On Friday nights, after testing negative for the rona, I'd go and visit an amazing friend, toting my small record collection with me. That's when I learned how to beatmatch and use DJ equipment.

Fast-forward, and I'm a dad who's been self-releasing music ever since. I've mastered other artists' music. I make my own album art. I've set up my own VJ installations for livestreaming. I love being able to share music I love and connect with people through DJing and the dancefloor. And while I'm drawn toward house and techno as my preferred genres to DJ, it's a true love and appreciation for artists who make and play groovy, soulful music that keeps this thing--whatever it is--going. 

- Kevin (aka K Priest)
